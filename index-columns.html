Latent BlendMar
Created and led research + development of Luma’s custom in-house image model for blending between images through embedding space.

Latent Blend is made for finding regions of image space that are ineffable by spherically interpolating between image embeddings, allowing nuanced, expressive, and novel forms to be discovered.

Resolved an ontology of exploration vs control, via injecting noise around an embedding, sweeps between locations or across parameters, and fine control by locking seeds and vector embeddings. Terrifically interesting, weird, and fun.Reference images are encoded into style embeddings, mixed by weight, and generated from the mix, so you can explore the space between images you already like instead of describing it in words. On the canvas a blend can be a weighted mix, a sweep from one image to another, or a seeded grid, with its weights and seed shown on every result so it can be read and repeated. The idea goes back to Xoromancy, navigating a GAN’s latent space with your hands.Most image models are steered with text, which makes it hard to explore the space between images you already like. Latent Blend encodes a set of reference images with DINOv3, a self-supervised vision model, mixes their embeddings by weight, and generates new images from the mix. The research ran for a year before it shipped: interpolation views, how style weights behave, what a seed should mean when you move between images. The interpolation itself is treated as design material, spherical rather than straight-line, with guidance rescaled and the math checked against a high-precision reference. On the canvas, a blend can be a weighted mix of several images, a sweep from one to another, or a grid of seeds. Every result carries the weights and seed that made it, so outputs can be read, compared and made again. The canvas agent can run blends too, with the same instructions a person follows. Making it something an artist can steer, repeat and compare was most of the work. It continues a thread from Xoromancy in 2019, where hand movements steered a GAN through its latent space. Shipped March 2026. Latent Blend’s grid sweep bilinearly interpolates between four source images in a plane. Scatter injects noise into each embedding, and the UI dots defocus as their samples drift off the plane into novel image space. Luma Agents
Ray3.2 ModifyJun
Principal design and fullstack engineering of the production-class Ray3.2 suite. Worked closely with research bringing model capabilities zero-to-one into the product, tuning conditioning modalities, to developing the overall user surface into hardened reliability. Established a new multikeyframe system with super-satisfying isotonically-optimal keyframe slot displacement from their compact timeline positions. Further established a standardized canvas-native panel system that durably locates the workspace and its results.Modify groups its controls by what they hold or change in the source video, the subject and the environment, so a clip can be restyled without losing what mattered in it. Create pins up to 64 image keyframes to exact output frames and previews them step by step on the canvas timeline. Reframe changes a video’s aspect ratio in an editor that snaps, and seeds carry through end to end so a result can be made again.It started as the Modify panel: controls grouped per conditioning signal, with nested advanced sections that other tools now use too. Guide frames followed, then Create, which adds text-to-video and up to 64 image keyframes pinned to the frames they should land on, previewed as steps and holds on the timeline. Reframe, for changing a video’s aspect ratio by outpainting, got its own on-canvas editor with snapping. Auto controls choose settings for you when you’d rather not. Seeds now carry from the controls through to the model and back, so any result can be reproduced or varied on purpose. After launch came a long stretch of making it dependable: frame numbers rounded the same way in the interface, the Python workers and the Go services, and fixes wherever the three disagreed. The same controls reach API partners, with seed control and recovery and PNG frame exports added for them. Luma Scenes builds its shots on Ray3.2 creates, and Modify Video was the first version of this idea in 2025. Luma Agents
Luma ScenesAug
Led design and engineering for Scenes, Luma’s custom video generation harness that autoregressively creates temporally-consistent Uni-1 keyframes. Given references and text instruction, an agent writes a multiscale, revisable scene where the resultant keyframes are editable on a dynamic, adjustable timeline.

Worked closely with Research to surface novel model capabilities, refine the harness + planner, and drive the productization 0-to-1.Clip editing lives in the track: trim, move, split, copy and paste, multi-select, and per-clip fit or fill, with resolution and frame-rate changes previewing live. Playback was rebuilt so the player, the video, hover and selection stop fighting each other, with frame-accurate timecode throughout. Scrubbing, stepping and looping work from the keyboard.Judging generated video means watching it closely, many times, so playback has to be exact. Early in the year came design and interaction passes on a windowed timeline editor: a frame-rate picker that keeps clip durations, more aspect ratios, frame-based timecode and tick labels, and a ghost playhead that previews where you’ll land. In June the canvas timeline got a full redesign with its other contributors. Clip editing moved into the track strip: trim, move, split, copy and paste, multi-select and per-clip fit or fill. Resolution and frame-rate changes preview live before they commit. In August, playback was rebuilt around one controller that owns its state, so the player library, the video element, hover and selection stopped fighting over it, with frame-rate-aware timecode and tests covering it. Scrubbing no longer freezes, and stepping and looping work from the keyboard. It’s the surface Luma Scenes retimes on and Ray3.2 Modify previews keyframes on.Ask for a change to any shot in plain language, drop in your own image for a keyframe (with a one-click restore), or drag keyframes and cuts to retime them. Renders run as independent jobs from the last approved version, so several can run at once and be compared. The canvas agent can take the same steps you can. With Eli and Lei.It began as a keyframes-only storyboard panel on the canvas, with a live strip of keyframes and a Ray3.2 create for each shot. Every keyframe is stamped with where it came from, so an image can always be traced back to its moment in the video. Review is built in: a keyframe or a segment can be revised by describing the change. Then the board became editable by hand. Any keyframe can be replaced by dropping in an image, with a one-click restore and an aspect-ratio pin so a mismatched image can’t reshape the video. Keyframes and cuts retime by dragging along the timeline. Around launch the product was renamed without breaking anything already saved. Video renders now run as independent jobs from the last approved revision, so several can overlap, and duplicates stay on that stable state while you keep editing. The canvas agent operates the same storyboard, so a story can be drafted by conversation and finished by hand. With Eli and Lei. Luma Agents
